# 2 Literature Review

## 2.1 Tail Latency and its Economic Significance

@dean2013tail observe that when a request fans out to $N$ parallel subsystems, each slow with probability $p$, the probability that at least one is slow is $1-(1-p)^N$, so modest per-component tail probabilities become near-certain tail events at scale, and the mean is a misleading summary. In high-frequency trading the effect is amplified because latency variance translates into adverse-selection cost, and @hasbrouck2013lowlatency established empirically, on NASDAQ data now nearly two decades old, that low-latency activity is a structural feature of equity markets. Regulation reinforces the point: RTS 25 under MiFID II requires venue members engaged in high-frequency algorithmic trading to synchronise business clocks to UTC within 100 microseconds [@ec2017rts25]. The quantity of interest is therefore the 99.9th percentile of tick-to-trade latency under representative load, which also bounds how accurate software timestamps can be. This dissertation measures its scheduling component.

## 2.2 Sources of Latency Variance in Linux

@boydwickizer2010analysis showed that kernel lock contention in the scheduler, the virtual file system, and the memory allocator dominates scalability on many-core systems, so latency variance is frequently an emergent property of contention rather than of one slow operation, although their 2010 measurements predate much of the lock work in later kernels. On NUMA hardware, @dashti2013traffic showed that congestion on memory controllers, not remote-access delay, explains most variance, so pinning memory close to a core is necessary but not sufficient. At the interrupt level, @herzog2018intspect showed on ARM and Intel platforms that interrupt handlers and user-space workloads interact to produce jitter, so a deferred interrupt appears as a tail event. Most pertinent is the PREEMPT_RT scheduling-latency bound of @bristot2020demystifying, which expresses worst-case latency as the sum of blocking and interference terms. Its value is that it applies to a kernel deployed in practice; its limitation is that a worst-case bound and an empirical percentile are different quantities and cannot validate each other directly.

## 2.3 Kernel-Resident Tuning Techniques

Several mechanisms are well documented individually but rarely evaluated in controlled combination. CPU isolation through cpuset removes housekeeping work from designated cores; IRQ affinity steers interrupts away from the application thread; nohz_full removes the periodic scheduling-clock interrupt on isolated cores [@intel2024realtime; @databento2024lowlatency]; and PREEMPT_RT, completed in mainline Linux 6.12, converts most spin-locks into preemptible mutexes and forces threaded interrupt handling [@bristot2020demystifying]. The evidence that these techniques work is largely grey literature. A recent engineering account of a cryptocurrency trading system, for example, reports a 99th-percentile matching latency under 25 microseconds at more than 100,000 messages per second per thread with critical threads pinned to dedicated cores and no kernel bypass [@liu2026hft], but such reports describe one system on one machine without a controlled baseline, and techniques are almost never evaluated together or in virtualised environments. This fragmentation motivates a factorial study in which the mechanisms are varied together so that their interactions can be estimated.

Two hardware effects interact with these mechanisms and are often left out of tuning guides. Under simultaneous multithreading (SMT), two hardware threads share one physical core's execution units, caches, and memory pipeline [@tullsen1995smt], so a latency-critical thread can be slowed by work on its sibling even when it has its logical CPU to itself. The second is processor idle states: a core with nothing to run enters a C-state in which parts of it are powered down, and waking from a deep state takes from a few to hundreds of microseconds. @schoene2015wakeup measured these latencies on x86 processors against the vendor specifications exposed to the operating system, which is what a tuning decision usually relies on, and real-time practice therefore limits idle states on latency-critical cores [@intel2024realtime]. An isolated core spends more of its time idle, so isolation and idle states are not independent, a point that becomes central in Chapter 6.

## 2.4 Kernel Bypass and its Contemporary Limits

Kernel-bypass frameworks dedicate a network interface to one application through poll-mode drivers, eliminating interrupt overhead and system-call transitions at the cost of an always-busy core [@belay2014ix; @majkowski2016kernel]. On raw latency alone bypass remains the strongest option, but its operational cost is often decisive outside co-location facilities: vendor-specific drivers, loss of standard observability, and limited cloud-provider support. The evidence on the size of the gap points in two directions. @jasny2025wakeup show that on 400 Gbit hardware neither sockets nor io_uring can saturate the link with small messages, with DPDK and RDMA handling each message in roughly 40 cycles against several thousand for the kernel path, and conclude that bypass remains necessary at that scale. @enow2022linux, in a non-peer-reviewed benchmark, found that kernel tuning narrowed DPDK's throughput advantage for an HTTP workload from about 4.2 times to about 1.5 times, and @lucey2023zerocopy present zero-copy receive through io_uring as a way to remove much of the remaining overhead. The disagreement is partly one of regime: the line rates of the first study are not those of a trading host handling a feed at tens of thousands of messages per second. Where the tuned kernel stands for that regime, under cloud constraints, is the long-term question of this line of work; the present dissertation does not measure it.

## 2.5 Precision Time Protocol and Measurement Fidelity

PTP, standardised as IEEE 1588-2019, uses hardware timestamping under assumptions of path symmetry [@ieee2019_1588], and Meta's production deployment illustrates the move from millisecond-level NTP to nanosecond-level PTP [@obleukhov2022ptp]. PTP improves the accuracy of latency measurement, not latency itself: with NTP-based timestamps Flow Traders observed apparent one-way latencies on AWS as low as minus 56 microseconds, an artefact eliminated by the PTP Hardware Clock, in a vendor-published case study with no independent replication [@aws2024flowtraders], now available on a wide range of EC2 instance types [@aws2024timesync]. In the planned cloud study (Appendix A), PTP is therefore instrumentation, not an intervention; the local study measures on a single clock and does not need it.

## 2.6 Automated Search over Tuning Configurations

The mechanisms above interact, and once a handful of them is combined the configuration space grows to thousands of points, each of which must be measured for many seconds before its tail percentiles can be trusted. Bayesian optimisation is designed for this setting: an objective that is costly to evaluate, observed with noise, and without useful gradients. A Gaussian-process surrogate is fitted to the evaluations so far, and an acquisition function such as expected improvement trades off exploitation against exploration [@jones1998efficient; @snoek2012practical; @shahriari2016taking]. CherryPick applied the idea to cloud configurations for analytics jobs and found near-optimal configurations far more cheaply than exhaustive search [@alipourfard2017cherrypick]. Random search is the natural control, because it is simple to parallelise and hard to beat when only a few parameters matter [@bergstra2012random].

Two cautions from this literature shape the study. First, a surrogate-guided search reports its best observed value, which under noise is optimistically biased, so the selected configuration must be re-measured independently before its performance is claimed; CherryPick itself reports best-found configurations without such a step. Second, conclusions about a search procedure drawn from one objective may not survive another, which argues for testing under deliberately perturbed assumptions [@sobol2001global]. Neither caution appears to have been applied to kernel tail-latency tuning.

## 2.7 Identified Research Gap

From this review, the evidence for kernel tuning is largely grey literature from single bare-metal systems without controlled baselines, which limits its applicability to cloud-first engineering; the formal latency-bound literature has been validated on real-time kernels, not on virtualised servers; the literature on cloud PTP consists largely of vendor case studies; and tuning choices are made by hand from checklists although the space is large, the mechanisms interact, and every evaluation is a noisy and expensive tail measurement, which is the setting automated search was designed for. The cloud question remains open and is the long-term goal of this line of work. No study known to the author has asked whether the configuration of such a system can be searched systematically with a small budget, and this dissertation addresses that gap empirically on commodity hardware.
