# 8 Conclusion

## 8.1 Summary of Findings

This dissertation began from the question of how far kernel-resident tuning, with cloud-provided precision time, can approach the tail latency of kernel bypass in a high-frequency trading context, and took the narrower step its resources allowed: 401 measurements on a commodity Linux workstation under heavy contention, a short explanatory experiment, and a simulation study with an exactly known optimum.

On RQ1, real-time scheduling is by far the most important mechanism on this platform: SCHED_FIFO reduced the 99.9th-percentile wake-up latency by a factor of about 32 and separated the scheduling classes completely. Freeing the SMT sibling of an isolated core reduced it by a further 45 per cent, whereas isolating the core by affinity alone raised the 99th percentile about six-fold under SCHED_OTHER; the follow-up experiment linked the isolation penalty to idle-state entry. On RQ2, both search methods found configurations with an observed 99.9th percentile below 20 microseconds within about 10 measurements; Bayesian optimisation wasted far fewer measurements on poor configurations and its re-measured picks had a median 99.9th percentile of 19 microseconds against 61 (A12 = 0.78), not significant with five seeds. H1 was supported and H2 was not refuted. In simulation, Bayesian optimisation came within 5 per cent of the optimum in 83 per cent of seeds with 20 evaluations, whereas random search reached that in about half of its seeds at 150, and on RQ3 that advantage held under every completed one-at-a-time perturbation.

## 8.2 Contributions

The dissertation contributes a per-mechanism account of the tail latency of a commodity Linux host under contention, based on full histograms and a balanced factorial design, which shows that isolation by affinity alone, the user-space stand-in for the boot-time isolation that practitioner guides recommend, raised the 99th percentile when the core was free to enter idle states. It also contributes a comparison of Bayesian optimisation and random search with independent re-measurement of every pick; a simulation study with an exact optimum and a perturbation analysis; and the published harness, tests, and raw logs from which every result can be regenerated.

## 8.3 Recommendations for Future Work

The limitations of Section 7.3 lead to six extensions. First, the power-management latency target and the deepest permitted idle state should become search parameters, which needs root but no kernel change. Second, kernel-level mechanisms (isolcpus, nohz_full, rcu_nocbs, IRQ affinity, PREEMPT_RT) should be added to the search space one at a time. Third, more seeds per method at the same budget are needed: eight would resolve the factor of two observed in Section 6.3, and twenty, about eleven hours of measurement, a factor of 1.5. Fourth, the remaining sensitivity models can be resumed from the saved checkpoints and the joint Sobol sweep started with the same command. Fifth, the cloud study of Appendix A would answer the questions this dissertation deferred: measurement fidelity with a PTP Hardware Clock, the analytical bound, and the residual gap to kernel bypass. Sixth, replaying an archived exchange feed in place of the synthetic load would test whether the ranking of mechanisms survives bursty, correlated arrivals.

## 8.4 Concluding Remarks

The main practical lesson is that the largest gains in tail latency on a commodity Linux host are cheap and quick to find: one setting, the real-time scheduling class, removed about 97 per cent of the 99.9th-percentile latency, and either search procedure found it within minutes. Two results qualify this. Isolating a core by affinity made the 99th percentile worse, and the follow-up linked the penalty to idle states; and the best observed value of a search understated the re-measured tail of its pick by a per-seed median factor of about 4 for random search and 1.6 for Bayesian optimisation. Re-measuring the selected configuration, checking mechanisms with a factorial design, and testing the search on a model with a known optimum were all needed to reach these conclusions; the cloud study should keep all three.
