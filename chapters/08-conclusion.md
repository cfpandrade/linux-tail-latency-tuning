# 8 Conclusion

## 8.1 Summary of Findings

This dissertation began from the question of how far kernel-resident tuning, combined with cloud-provided precision time, can approach the tail latency of kernel bypass in a high-frequency trading context. That question was beyond the resources of the project, so the dissertation took a narrower step that real evidence could support: 401 measurements of 30 seconds each on a commodity Linux workstation under heavy contention, a short explanatory experiment, and a simulation study with an exactly known optimum.

On RQ1, real-time scheduling is by far the most important mechanism on this platform. SCHED_FIFO reduced the 99.9th-percentile wake-up latency by a factor of about 32 and separated the scheduling classes completely. Freeing the SMT sibling of an isolated core reduced it by a further 45 per cent. Isolating the core by affinity alone raised the 99th percentile about six-fold under SCHED_OTHER, an effect that the follow-up experiment linked to idle-state entry. Neither pinning nor the choice of CPU made a detectable difference once SCHED_FIFO was used. On RQ2, both search methods found configurations meeting the success criterion within about 10 measurements. Bayesian optimisation spent 85 per cent of its budget on SCHED_FIFO configurations against 43 per cent for random search, and its re-measured picks had a median 99.9th percentile of 19 microseconds against 61 (A12 = 0.78), a difference not significant with five seeds. H1 was supported, since every pick reduced the pooled 99.9th percentile by at least a factor of 37; H2 was not refuted. In simulation, Bayesian optimisation came within 5 per cent of the optimum in 83 per cent of seeds with 20 evaluations, whereas random search needed 150 for its median regret to fall below 5 per cent. On RQ3, that advantage was unchanged under every completed one-at-a-time perturbation, and the most important parameter was the same in every model. The simulation also explains the real result: when one parameter dominates, a small random sample almost surely contains a good configuration, and the advantage of model-based search appears in how the budget is spent rather than in the final pick.

## 8.2 Contributions

The dissertation contributes a measured, per-mechanism account of the tail latency of a commodity Linux host under contention, based on full histograms and a balanced factorial design, which shows that isolation by affinity alone, a step recommended early in practitioner guides, raised the 99th percentile when the processor was free to enter idle states; a comparison of Bayesian optimisation and random search for tuning a latency-critical host in which every pick was re-measured independently; a simulation study with an exact optimum and a perturbation analysis that quantifies how the advantage of Bayesian optimisation depends on the budget; and the harness itself, with its tests, raw logs, and an outline of the cloud study, which allow every result in Chapter 6 to be regenerated and the study to be extended.

## 8.3 Recommendations for Future Work

The limitations of Section 7.3 lead to six extensions, ordered by how directly they follow from the results. First, idle-state control: the power-management latency target and the deepest permitted idle state should be added as search parameters, which needs root but no kernel change. Second, kernel-level mechanisms: isolcpus, nohz_full, rcu_nocbs, IRQ affinity, and PREEMPT_RT should be added to the search space one at a time so that their effect can be compared with the user-space mechanisms measured here. Third, more seeds: twenty seeds per method at the same budget would take about twelve hours and give the test of H2 useful power. Fourth, the remaining sensitivity models and the joint Sobol sweep can be resumed from the saved checkpoints. Fifth, the cloud study of Appendix A, which would answer the questions the proposal set out and this dissertation deferred: measurement fidelity with a PTP Hardware Clock, the analytical bound, and the residual gap to kernel bypass. Sixth, a realistic workload: replaying an archived exchange feed in place of the synthetic load would test whether the ranking of mechanisms survives bursty, correlated arrivals.

## 8.4 Concluding Remarks

The main practical lesson is that the largest gains in tail latency on a commodity Linux host are cheap and quick to find. One setting, the real-time scheduling class, removed about 97 per cent of the 99.9th-percentile latency, and either search procedure found it within minutes. Two results qualify this. Isolating a core by affinity made the 99th percentile worse unless the core was kept out of idle states, because power management then became the main source of delay. And the best observed value of a search understated the re-measured tail of its pick by a factor of up to about 6. Re-measuring the selected configuration, checking mechanisms with a factorial design, and testing the search on a model with a known optimum were all needed to reach these conclusions, and the cloud study should keep all three.
