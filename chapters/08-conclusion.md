# 8 Conclusion

## 8.1 Summary of Findings

This dissertation began from the question of how far kernel-resident tuning, combined with cloud-provided precision time, can approach the tail latency of kernel bypass in a high-frequency trading context. That question was beyond the resources of the project, so the dissertation took a narrower step that real evidence could support: 401 measurements on a commodity Linux workstation under heavy contention, a short explanatory experiment, and a simulation study with an exactly known optimum.

On RQ1, real-time scheduling is by far the most important mechanism on this platform: SCHED_FIFO reduced the 99.9th-percentile wake-up latency by a factor of about 32 and separated the scheduling classes completely. Freeing the SMT sibling of an isolated core reduced it by a further 45 per cent, whereas isolating the core by affinity alone raised the 99th percentile about six-fold under SCHED_OTHER, an effect the follow-up experiment linked to idle-state entry. On RQ2, both search methods found configurations meeting the success criterion within about 10 measurements; Bayesian optimisation spent its budget far better and its re-measured picks had a median 99.9th percentile of 19 microseconds against 61 (A12 = 0.78), a difference not significant with five seeds. H1 was supported and H2 was not refuted. In simulation, Bayesian optimisation came within 5 per cent of the optimum in 83 per cent of seeds with 20 evaluations, whereas random search needed 150, and on RQ3 that advantage held under every completed one-at-a-time perturbation. When one parameter dominates, a small random sample almost surely contains a good configuration, and the advantage of model-based search appears in how the budget is spent rather than in the final pick.

## 8.2 Contributions

The dissertation contributes a per-mechanism account of the tail latency of a commodity Linux host under contention, based on full histograms and a balanced factorial design, which shows that isolation by affinity alone, a step recommended early in practitioner guides, raised the 99th percentile when the processor was free to enter idle states; a comparison of Bayesian optimisation and random search in which every pick was re-measured independently; a simulation study with an exact optimum and a perturbation analysis; and the harness itself, with its tests and raw logs, published so that every result can be regenerated.

## 8.3 Recommendations for Future Work

The limitations of Section 7.3 lead to six extensions, ordered by how directly they follow from the results. First, the power-management latency target and the deepest permitted idle state should be added as search parameters, which needs root but no kernel change. Second, kernel-level mechanisms (isolcpus, nohz_full, rcu_nocbs, IRQ affinity, PREEMPT_RT) should be added to the search space one at a time. Third, twenty seeds per method at the same budget would take about twelve hours and give the test of H2 useful power. Fourth, the remaining sensitivity models and the joint Sobol sweep can be resumed from the saved checkpoints. Fifth, the cloud study of Appendix A would answer the questions this dissertation deferred: measurement fidelity with a PTP Hardware Clock, the analytical bound, and the residual gap to kernel bypass. Sixth, replaying an archived exchange feed in place of the synthetic load would test whether the ranking of mechanisms survives bursty, correlated arrivals.

## 8.4 Concluding Remarks

The main practical lesson is that the largest gains in tail latency on a commodity Linux host are cheap and quick to find: one setting, the real-time scheduling class, removed about 97 per cent of the 99.9th-percentile latency, and either search procedure found it within minutes. Two results qualify this. Isolating a core by affinity made the 99th percentile worse unless the core was kept out of idle states, and the best observed value of a search understated the re-measured tail of its pick by a factor of up to about 6. Re-measuring the selected configuration, checking mechanisms with a factorial design, and testing the search on a model with a known optimum were all needed to reach these conclusions, and the cloud study should keep all three.
