# 1 Introduction

## 1.1 Context and Motivation

In electronic markets, trading firms compete on latencies measured in microseconds. @hasbrouck2013lowlatency, analysing NASDAQ order data from 2007 and 2008, found low-latency responses clustering at two to three milliseconds, and reaction times have compressed by orders of magnitude since. At this scale the mean says little; the tail of the latency distribution matters more, because, as @dean2013tail showed for large-scale services, a rare slow response becomes a common one at scale. For a market-making engine a single outlier of a few hundred microseconds during a volatility spike can invalidate a live quote, expose the firm to adverse selection, or corrupt software timestamps that Regulatory Technical Standard 25 under MiFID II requires to be accurate to within 100 microseconds [@ec2017rts25]. The quantity that matters is a tight, measurable bound on the 99th and 99.9th percentiles of tick-to-trade latency under representative load.

## 1.2 Problem Statement

The Linux kernel exhibits latency variance that is acceptable for general-purpose workloads and unacceptable for latency-critical ones; documented sources include interrupt coalescing, softirq deferral, frequency scaling, scheduler migration, NUMA memory-controller contention, and the periodic scheduling-clock interrupt [@bristot2020demystifying; @herzog2018intspect; @dashti2013traffic]. The established industry response is kernel bypass: frameworks such as DPDK, RDMA, and AF_XDP dedicate a network interface to one application, trading protection and portability for deterministic packet delivery [@belay2014ix; @majkowski2016kernel].

Two developments motivate re-examining this trade-off. First, engineering evidence suggests that a carefully tuned kernel recovers much of the throughput that bypass promises [@enow2022linux], and io_uring offers a hybrid path that keeps kernel abstractions while removing most context-switch overhead [@lucey2023zerocopy], although at the highest line rates bypass remains clearly ahead [@jasny2025wakeup]. Second, precise time synchronisation is now a cloud service: AWS exposes a PTP Hardware Clock to guest instances with microsecond accuracy [@aws2024timesync; @aws2024flowtraders], which makes one-way latency between cloud machines measurable with a clock independent of the system under test.

A further problem is how a configuration is chosen. The tuning mechanisms interact, each evaluation is a noisy measurement of a rare event, and in practice the configuration is set by hand from checklists [@intel2024realtime; @databento2024lowlatency]. Whether automated search can do better with few measurements has not, to the author's knowledge, been tested for kernel tail latency, and the interaction of cloud-provided time synchronisation with kernel-resident tuning has not been characterised in the peer-reviewed literature.

## 1.3 Scope, Aim, and Contributions

The question that motivates this work is whether kernel-level tuning, combined with cloud-provided PTP synchronisation, can approach the tail-latency behaviour of kernel bypass in a high-frequency trading context. Answering it in full requires bare-metal cloud instances with a PTP Hardware Clock, a real-time kernel, and many instance-hours of measurement, which were not available to this MSc project, so the dissertation takes a narrower, feasible step. Its aim is to establish, on a commodity Linux workstation under controlled contention, how much of the scheduling-latency tail can be removed by the tuning mechanisms that need no kernel reconfiguration, and whether a good combination of those mechanisms can be found automatically within a small measurement budget and confirmed by independent re-measurement. The research questions and hypotheses are stated in Chapter 3. The workload is synthetic, for reasons of reproducibility and research ethics set out in Section 4.9, and the design of the cloud study is outlined in Appendix A.

The dissertation makes four contributions: a per-mechanism characterisation on real hardware of the tail-latency effect of each user-space mechanism and of the policy-dependence of those effects, based on full latency histograms; a comparison of Bayesian optimisation and random search as tuning procedures in which every selected configuration is re-measured, so that the comparison is not biased by lucky measurements; a simulation study with an exactly known optimum, which quantifies how the advantage of Bayesian optimisation depends on the budget and tests that conclusion under perturbation; and a reproducible measurement harness with its tests and raw logs, published with this dissertation.

Chapter 2 reviews the literature and states the research gap, Chapter 3 the aim, questions, and hypotheses, Chapter 4 the methodology, Chapter 5 the implementation, Chapter 6 the results, Chapter 7 the discussion, and Chapter 8 the conclusions and future work.
