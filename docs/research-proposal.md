# Research Proposal (source document)

Deterministic Low-Latency Optimisation in Cloud-Hosted Linux Systems: A Hybrid Empirical and Analytical Study of Kernel Tuning and PTP Synchronisation for High-Frequency Trading.

Author: Carlos Andrade. Archived here as the reference source for the dissertation (abstract, introduction, literature review II.A to II.F, research questions RQ1 to RQ4, methodology, expected outcomes, and the 24 references).

## Abstract

High-frequency trading (HFT) systems operate in a regime where tail latency, specifically the 99th and 99.9th percentiles rather than mean latency, governs profitability and regulatory compliance. While kernel-bypass technologies such as DPDK and RDMA dominate on-premises HFT infrastructure, their operational complexity, hardware lock-in, and historical incompatibility with public-cloud environments motivate a renewed examination of kernel-resident optimisation paths. The recent availability of Precision Time Protocol Hardware Clocks (PHC) in AWS Nitro provides, for the first time, sub-microsecond timestamping without customer-owned grand-master hardware, yet the interaction of cloud PHC with Linux kernel tuning has not been systematically characterised in peer-reviewed literature. This proposal presents a hybrid empirical and analytical research plan that quantifies the contribution of IRQ affinity, CPU isolation, NUMA-aware placement, tickless operation, and PREEMPT_RT to tail-latency reduction in cloud-hosted Linux, under a synthetic HFT tick-to-trade workload. A scheduling-latency bound derived from the Thread Synchronisation Model of PREEMPT_RT is instantiated with empirically measured parameters, and PHC-based hardware timestamps are used to bound measurement error below the target effect size. Expected contributions include a cloud-specific tuning methodology validated against an analytical bound, a reproducible open-source benchmark harness, and a quantified residual gap with respect to published kernel-bypass baselines, with implications for regulated financial infrastructure under MiFID II Regulatory Technical Standard 25.
